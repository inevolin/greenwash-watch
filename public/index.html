Green claims, checked against evidence.

Greenwashing means making a product or company look more environmentally friendly than the evidence supports. Greenwash Watch is an AI agent that reads a brand's own claims, looks for independent evidence, and gives each claim an assessment: holds up, unsubstantiated or misleading. Every assessment links to its sources.

Why it matters

Misleading green claims make it hard for people to choose more sustainable products, and they undercut companies whose claims are honest. These figures come from regulators:

1 · Find

The agent uses web_search to find the brand's recent claims, and quotes them word for word.

2 · Verify

It uses web_fetch to read reports, NGO analyses and rulings, and needs at least one independent source per claim.

3 · Assess

It gives each claim one of three verdicts, worded as "our assessment" and never as an accusation, and remembers each brand between runs.

The rules of the game

In force

EU Directive 2024/825

The "Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition" directive applies from 27 Sep 2026. It bans generic claims (such as "eco-friendly") that lack proof of recognised excellent environmental performance, "neutral" claims based on offsets, and sustainability labels without certification.

EUR-Lex ↗
Guidance

UK CMA Green Claims Code

The code sets six principles: claims must be truthful and accurate, clear and unambiguous, must not omit or hide information, must make fair comparisons, must consider the full life cycle, and must be substantiated.

GOV.UK ↗
Stalled

EU Green Claims Directive

This is still a proposal (COM(2023)166). The Commission announced plans to withdraw it in June 2025, but it has not been formally withdrawn. Parliament's tracker lists it as "Blocked".
